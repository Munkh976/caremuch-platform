Many families access Respite Care and Community Living Supports through Michigan Medicaid programs, which can cover the cost of these services for eligible individuals. Foster parents may also qualify for respite support through Medicaid or foster care services.

Getting started involves several steps. First, check eligibility. Respite and CLS services may be available for youth with disabilities who qualify for Michigan Medicaid, families enrolled in the Children's Waiver Program (CWP) or other Home and Community-Based Services (HCBS), youth receiving services through their local Community Mental Health agency, and foster parents who may be eligible depending on the child's needs.

Second, connect with your local Community Mental Health agency. Families typically begin by contacting their local Community Mental Health agency, whose intake team guides you through the eligibility and service process.

Third, complete an assessment. A Community Mental Health agency support coordinator or case manager completes an assessment to determine your child's needs and which services are appropriate.

Fourth, request Ripple Effects Community Inclusion Center as your provider. During your person-centered planning meeting with your Community Mental Health agency, you can request that your Respite or CLS referral be sent to Ripple Effects as your service provider.

Fifth, receive service authorization. Once services are approved, your case manager notifies you of the number of authorized hours and sends the referral to Ripple Effects.

Sixth, schedule your first session. After we receive the referral, our team contacts you to welcome your family, learn about your child's strengths, needs, and interests, schedule your first respite or CLS session, and create a personalized support plan.

Local Community Mental Health agencies serving our area include Integrated Services of Kalamazoo (Kalamazoo County), Van Buren Community Mental Health (Van Buren County), Pivotal (St. Joseph County), OnPoint (Allegan County), and Riverwood Center (Berrien County). Your local agency's intake or access team is the first point of contact to begin services.
